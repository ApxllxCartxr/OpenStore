# OpenStore Rebuild Spec (v1)

Sidecar-only rebuild. Same premise as before, solid requirements, guardrails on from commit one. Glossary in `CONTEXT.md` is law; decisions in `docs/adr/` are law.

## 1. Surfaces (3 roots)

- `src/openstore/sidecar/` — the product. Makes its Merchant site transactable by any Buyer Agent for Consumers. One deploy serves one Merchant domain only (ADR-0007). Middleware only: no product info of its own, no shopping UI. Layout: `src/openstore/sidecar/`.
- `demo/merchant-site/` — SpoiledDuckie demo accessory store (Surface 2). Next.js + Postgres, own container, own DB. Real shop: home/shop/item/direct-checkout/order-lookup storefront plus shop-ops admin (dashboard, catalog + variants/media/CSV, inventory moves audit, orders Timeline + refund queue). Manually-set prices only — no offers/discounts *engine* (cut per Q44) — plus the pricing the law and the post office require: shipping zones with flat rates, GST rates per item (GSTIN, HSN/SAC, CGST/SGST vs IGST by place of supply), and a hand-entered discount code table with no rules behind it. It serves these through door 9 `quote` (ADR-0010) and sends its own order-confirmation, refund, and new-order notifications. Proves a Merchant can operate it.
- `demo/buyer-chat/` — demo Buyer Agent (Surface 3). Claude-like chat with tool calls over Ollama (swappable), air-gapped stranger: own folder/process/deps/DB, zero sidecar imports, HTTP-only, admitted like any external agent by its own published Agent Profile (ADR-0012) with Merchant-issued OAuth as the allowlisted alternative. Proves the Consumer flow end to end.
- Build-breaking import firewall both directions (runtime + types + tests). CI red on any crossing.

## 2. Actors (CONTEXT.md)

Merchant (human, owns store/keys/catalog/policies) / Consumer (human, buys, pays manually every time) / Buyer Agent (any external agent, proposal-only, holding no spending authority — a self-registered stranger signs its own requests with its own key (ADR-0012), but never holds a payment credential and never authorizes a spend) / Payment Provider (the Merchant's: Justpay, Airpay, Razorpay via one trait in v1; untrusted, never decides authority). One sidecar = one Merchant (ADR-0007).

"Any Buyer Agent" is a statement about the *admission mechanism*, not about reach: self-registration accepts any agent that speaks the protocol, and today that is a hand-rolled MCP client, not a mainstream assistant surface — ChatGPT and Perplexity source shopping catalogues through feed submission and gated merchant programmes, neither of which pulls an arbitrary merchant-hosted endpoint. Which agents speak it is a distribution problem tracked in [`PLAN-distribution.md`](./PLAN-distribution.md), not a limitation of the Gate. Install-facing copy must carry this distinction rather than inherit the unqualified claim (`docs/REVIEW-remediation.md` item #3).

The Payment Provider being *the Merchant's own account* is load-bearing beyond this section: ADR-0021's ECO/TCS boundary and the payment-orchestration boundary both rest on it, and any change here reopens those ADRs rather than being a configuration detail.

## 3. Discovery (ADR-0005)

V1 = direct-add: Consumer pastes Merchant URL, agent fetches the sidecar card (`/.well-known/agent-commerce.json` + UCP manifest + JWKS) directly. List-file format (`lists/seed.json`: name/category/city/sidecar URL/public key/protocols) ships scaffolded (empty + example) for later; no crawler, ranking, or hosted search in v1. A mall later reads the same format with zero money-core changes. Reach without a mall, in the two formats that are actually ingested: the Merchant site emits schema.org `Product` + `Offer` JSON-LD on its product pages (what crawlers and AI channels read, and it belongs with the pages that own the SEO), and the sidecar serves `/agent/feed.json` over the exposed catalogue using Google Merchant Center product-data attribute names (`id`, `item_group_id`, `title`, `description`, `link`, `image_link`, `availability`, `price`, `brand`, `condition`, `color`, `size`, `gtin`/`mpn` where known) — where `availability` takes the reader's published enum and never the Availability Bucket verbatim, since `low-stock` is not a member of it and folds into in-stock, and a feed carrying an invented value is rejected by the only reader that matters — one feed item per Catalogue Item, grouped by `item_group_id`, which is how variants are expressed in every feed that matters (schema.org's equivalent is `ProductGroup` + `hasVariant`) so a Merchant can submit it by hand without a translation step. Inventing a third private schema would produce a file nobody ingests. The sidecar never crawls, ranks, or submits on the Merchant's behalf.

## 4. Money core (ADR-0002)

One pipeline, four stages, every protocol hits it:
1. **Cart + Quote** — basket slip `{sku, qty, price}` where every `sku` is a Catalogue Item, i.e. one resolved variant (black tote, size M) and never a Product Group; an agent that sends a group refuses with `variant-required` rather than having a size guessed for it. Plus, before any spend, a Destination, a Contact Point, and a chosen Fulfillment Option. The Merchant prices the whole thing through door 9 `quote` and returns the Quote: subtotal, discount lines (a Merchant Discount Code, validated and consumed by the Merchant and never interpreted here — `public` codes ride in from the Buyer Agent's checkout card, `private` codes are refused there and entered only on the approve page, ADR-0015), fulfillment options with costs, tax lines (GST — CGST/SGST or IGST by **per-line** Place of Supply, with GSTIN and HSN/SAC on the invoice: goods take the Destination state, a service sold at the premises takes the place it is performed, so one basket holding a tote and a charm-bar seat can legitimately carry IGST on one line and CGST/SGST on the other; an Add-on is a composite supply and never a line of its own — its amount folds into its parent's taxable value and inherits the parent's rate, HSN/SAC, and Place of Supply, so gift-wrap on a charm-bar seat is taxed as that service, and an Add-on arriving without a parent refuses with `addon-without-parent`), and total. The Quote carries no validity of its own: the sidecar already owns both expiry clocks (§5), so the sidecar issues the checkout expiry that `expiry_utc` binds and that the agent renders as a countdown, and door 9 stays a pure pricing answer with no clock in it. The sidecar ships no rate table, tax engine, or discount engine (ADR-0010). A Pending Cart holds ₹0 with an expiry and is distinct from a `pending` order row. Dry-run compile available with zero side effects.
2. **Compiler (Gate)** — deterministic checklist only: authority-present-and-accepted + currency/Merchant/window/count/qty/blocked/tags/caps/quote-fresh/method-enabled in fixed order (the Authority kind must be one the Merchant enabled, and the Transcript records which kind authorized and what it bound, ADR-0017) (count/qty/caps evaluate at the Product Group, so buying two of each colour cannot walk through a two-per-order cap), all failures recorded in the Transcript with `reason_code` = first failure, closed reason codes, byte-stable transcript stored with the decision. `cart_hash` = sha256 of canonical bytes: UTF-8 JSON with sorted keys, no whitespace, of `{lines: [{sku, qty, price_minor}] sorted by sku, quote_hash, destination_hash, contact_hash, fulfillment_option_id, total_minor, currency, merchant_domain, expiry_utc}` — the Consumer authorizes the delivered total, not the item total. Any change to what the preimage covers produces a fresh `cart_hash`; whether it also owes a fresh Authority follows that Authority's Binding (ADR-0017), so a `passkey` bound the cart and any change re-taps, while a `upi-pin` bound the amount and only `total_minor` or `currency` moving does. The gap that leaves is closed on display rather than on hashes: anything the Consumer was shown on the approve page is covered, and a change after render invalidates the token, so a Destination edit that does not move the total cannot redirect a parcel someone already paid for. `quote_hash` = sha256 of the canonical Quote bytes (all breakdown lines sorted by type then label, minor units, sign intrinsic); `destination_hash` / `contact_hash` = `HMAC-SHA256(order_salt, canonical PII bytes)` where the 128-bit `order_salt` lives **only in the Merchant order row and never in the bundle** — erasure takes the salt with the row and the commitment can no longer be opened (a bare hash of a phone number is 10^10 guesses, i.e. no protection at all) (ADR-0011). Frozen after ADR-0010 lands and before S3/S6, so translators produce byte-identical core transcripts. Quote-fresh runs **once, immediately before `RESERVE`** (the confirm that follows the tap): re-call `quote` with identical inputs, byte-compare against the pinned Quote, and on drift fail closed with `price-changed` so the Consumer re-taps the new number. It never runs after payment — once money has moved the pinned Quote is frozen and the only post-payment check is the Provider amount/currency reconcile (`amount-mismatch`), because failing a paid order over a Merchant price edit would strand real money against no order. The Gate also verifies the Quote's *arithmetic* — lines sum to the total, signs correct (discounts negative), currency matches, subtotal equals `sum(qty × attested price)` from the pinned Attestation, tax lines additive or informational per the Merchant's tax-inclusive flag — refusing `quote-inconsistent` if they disagree. Checking a Merchant's sums is not computing prices; without it a buggy or compromised Merchant gets its total signed unchallenged. Authority does not always arrive at the same moment — before the Gate under `passkey`, with the money under `upi-pin` — so the Gate's two jobs separate (ADR-0017): `decide()` runs every check including quote-fresh, writes the Transcript, and permits the payment request; `settle()` verifies the Provider record, records the Authority with its Binding, and captures. Policy is Merchant-owned (edited in `/agentic`, enforced here). Agent output never executes; this re-validates server-side every time.
3. **Ledger** — sidecar-owned append-only Ledger `RESERVE → CAPTURE / RELEASE / REFUND / REVERSAL`, every money entry carrying `amount_minor`. Two invariants, deliberately separate: **holds close** (each `RESERVE` ends in exactly one `CAPTURE` or `RELEASE`; open holds sum to zero once closed — this is the escrow-zero rule and it says nothing about cash) and **refunds are bounded** (`sum(REFUND) ≤ captured − already_refunded`). A `REVERSAL` — money removed by someone other than the Merchant as the Provider reports it: an adjudicated UPI complaint, a post-settlement adjustment, a bank correction, or a card chargeback — is bounded by neither and is recorded even when it drives the order's net cash negative, because refusing to record money that has already left is how books go wrong; negative net raises an alert, never a refusal. Paise integers only, UTC. One idempotency key per attempt (`order_id:attempt`) with a unique constraint closes read-then-act races — except at `orders.create`, which is the door that *produces* `order_id`, and which keys on `cart_id:attempt` instead, the Pending Cart being the thing an order is made from; a crash between request and response is exactly where duplicate orders are born; crash-mid-link adopts via `check-status`, never double-creates. Pending holds no money; tap converts, never auto-converts.
4. **Evidence** — tight 5-section sealed receipt (bought / tapped / decided / told / moved): hash-chained, Merchant-ES256-signed, no Merkle in v1 (ADR-0009). Carries its JWKS snapshot so offline verify is really offline; opens by unguessable 128-bit receipt ID, no login. Each order pins the Attestation hash it used so later edits can't rewrite history. Refunds version the receipt: the original bundle stays verifiable as-is; a refund appends a new `moved` entry (`REFUND`) + fresh signature as v2. Attestation `digest` = sha256 of canonical per-item bytes (`sku | price_minor | sorted tags | canonical options JSON`, UTF-8, frozen before S3/S6), where the options JSON is the Catalogue Item's *resolved* values (`{colour: black, size: M}`) — so a later rename of an option axis cannot rewrite what was bought.

## 5. Merchant truth + stock trait (ADR-0001, Q40)

Merchant system owns Product Groups, Catalogue Items, stock counts, and order rows. Stock, price, and thresholds live on the Catalogue Item — the variant — never on the group: one count for "the tote" would let black selling out mark red sold out and would point `reserve`'s compare-and-set at the wrong row. Sidecar reads fresh at every Gate and mirrors back idempotently. Narrow HTTP trait only — 9 doors: `catalog.read (1) / stock.read (2) / reserve (3) / commit (4) / release (5) / restock (6) / orders.create+read (7) / orders.set-status (8) / quote (9)`, HMAC + idempotency keys, no shared DB ever (demo included). Door 9 is read-only and side-effect-free: same inputs, same bytes, any number of times (ADR-0010). Sidecar owns the expiry clock and calls `orders.set-status(expired)`; the Merchant never self-expires; tap vs expiry serialize on `set-status` with same-key-same-result. `.yaml` counts exist only where the basic site *is* the Merchant system.
- Stock: always `int ≥ 0`, never null, with DB `CHECK (available >= 0)`. Missing/negative/float fails catalog load loud. `0` = refuse with `sold-out`; v1 never oversells (`continue-selling-when-out-of-stock` = false). Exact counts are Merchant-and-sidecar-internal: door 2 `stock.read` returns integers over the private network, but everything agent-facing returns an Availability Bucket (`in-stock` / `low-stock` / `sold-out`) per Catalogue Item, cut at that item's own low-stock threshold, with a Product Group showing in-stock when any of its items is, because an exact count handed to any self-registered stranger is both competitive intelligence and an inventory-probing oracle. One deliberate exception, taken with eyes open against the smoothness law (§11): a quantity refusal the Consumer is actively waiting on names the exact remaining count, since "try fewer" without a number is not a fix — that path is rate-limited and counted like any other oracle. Reserve is atomic compare-and-set (`WHERE available >= qty`), never negative-proof by test. `failed` auto-restores stock it reduced (Woo 11.0 parity). Services sell countable slots. Restock = Merchant edit. A refund restores only the lines whose restock flag was ticked; a refund with none ticked moves no stock.
- External (non-agent) sales win automatically: the next gate re-reads the lowered `available` (Shopify `available`/`committed`/`reserved` + webhooks; Woo `stock_quantity` + hold-stock). Events are triggers only — unordered, duplicated, never guaranteed — so the sidecar dedupes by `event_id`, re-fetches fresh, and runs a periodic reconciler poll.

## 6. Orders — 8 canonical statuses (Q41)

`pending` (agent cart, ₹0 moved and **no stock held** — the 24h window is the Quote's validity, not an inventory hold, which is why nothing is reserved until the tap. A Merchant row, distinct from a Pending Cart; only sidecar-created agent orders auto-expire, admin/manual orders never do) / `confirmed` (Consumer initiated payment — the Authority has either already landed (`passkey`) or arrives with the money (`upi-pin`, ADR-0017) — payment request out, stock held by `RESERVE` — and therefore time-boxed: the sidecar releases at the link's own expiry, default 15 min and never longer than the Provider's link lifetime. An abandoned tap must not hold stock; Woo's `hold-stock` default is 60 min and a UPI collect request expires in minutes, so leaving `confirmed` open for 24h would be an inventory-denial hole any self-registered stranger agent could open at will) / `paid` / `cancelled` (pre-money only: `pending/confirmed` → `cancelled` by Consumer walk-away/decline or shop reject with reason; frees hold via `RELEASE`, kills UPI link via `cancel`; hidden once `completed`, like Shopify) / `expired` (two sweeps, one status: `pending` at 24h with `time-limit-reached` and untouched stock — that window is a Quote's validity, not a stock hold — and `confirmed` at the payment-link expiry with `payment-window-elapsed` plus a `RELEASE` returning the held stock; re-add to retry) / `failed` (two causes with different ledger consequences and they must not be written as one: `sold-out` is door 3 refusing, so no `RESERVE` ever existed and none is closed — releasing a hold that was never taken is refused by the trait; `amount-mismatch` arrives after a successful `RESERVE` and does auto-`RELEASE` it. Both restore any stock this order reduced) / `refunded` (post-money only: `paid/completed` → `refunded` by shop click with reason + per-line restock yes/no; partial amounts allowed — the refund carries `amount_minor ≤ captured − already_refunded`, enforced by the Ledger, and full vs partial is *derived* from `refunded_minor` rather than given a ninth status, so the canonical set stays 8 and every adapter mapping holds; fees, cancellation charges, and store credit stay phase-two) / `completed` (Merchant-fulfilled, observed). Dispatch is a Merchant-recorded event, not a ninth status and not gated to any single status: recording an optional `tracking_number` + `carrier` sets `dispatched_at` and, on the first call for the order, assigns the sequential `invoice_number` (financial-year-scoped, gapless, atomic increment, distinct from the unguessable `receipt_id` the bundle opens by, since a legal tax invoice and an IDOR-proof lookup key need opposite properties; ADR-0020) — CGST Rule 46 ties the invoice to removal of the goods, not to payment, so this fires at dispatch rather than at `paid`, and can land while a COD order is still `confirmed`, before `paid` (cash collected at delivery, ADR-0018) exists at all. Both fields are Merchant-entered, non-PII, mirrored through door 7 `orders.create+read`, surfaced in the Consumer's polled order status same as any other field, and fire their own shipped notification off the Merchant's own comms channel — SPEC §10 already owns that job, this just adds an event to the set it sends — independently of the later `completed` transition, which stays the Merchant's own closure signal. COD runs the same eight (ADR-0018): `confirmed` means the intent is verified and stock is held by door 3 with no money held at all; `paid` is the cash collected at delivery; an RTO is `cancelled` with an `rto` reason and a restock, which is already what `cancelled` means — pre-money, stock returned. `expires_at` takes a third deadline there, the delivery window. Refund needs no return: the per-line restock checkboxes decide stock return. Timeline and receipt show `Refunded ₹X of ₹Y` so partial is legible without a new status. Refund requests share one queue (`refund-requested` from agent via sidecar, or recorded manually for direct buyers); same button settles both. Status flip alone never moves money (Woo parity). Every status change lands in the order Timeline (Transcript + `stock_moves` + status history). Adapters map 1:1 to Shopify (`pending/authorized/paid/voided/expired/refunded`, `unfulfilled/fulfilled`) and Woo (`pending/on-hold/processing/completed/cancelled/failed/refunded` + hold-stock timeout), with one honest seam: a partial refund maps to Shopify `partially_refunded` and to Woo plain `refunded` (Woo has no partial status), so the adapter carries `refunded_minor` alongside the status rather than pretending the target platform can express it. Destination, Contact Point, and the line's resolved option values ride inside the order row in v1 — Destination and Contact Point as the only plaintext copy (ADR-0011), the options as a denormalised copy for history matching the pinned Attestation. Core stays platform-blind.

## 7. Authority: passkeys + scoped agent keys (ADR-0004, Q28/Q53)

- Consumer: a fresh Authority per spend, one member of the closed set `upi-pin / passkey / confirmed-intent / mandate` (ADR-0017), accepted only where the Merchant enabled that kind and recorded with what it bound. In India the default is `upi-pin`: the payer authenticates in their own PSP app against a named payee and an exact amount, so the amount is bound by the payer's own bank and the basket is bound by reference through the Quote the approve page rendered. `mandate` is defined and refused in v1. The `passkey` member is the strongest binding and the original ceremony: fresh passkey tap per spend, bound to exact `cart_hash` + amount + expiry (UV on). Enrollment is per Merchant domain (ADR-0008: roaming five stores = five enrollments). Platform keys on laptop/mobile, QR cross-device. Enrollment = login; no passwords/OTPs/magic links to money. Enrollment is never a separate visit: a first-time Consumer enrolls *inside* the first approve ceremony — one page, one biometric prompt, enroll-and-tap — so roaming five stores costs five taps, not five signup flows. Mechanically this is a single WebAuthn `create()` whose challenge **is** the `cart_hash` + amount + expiry binding with UV required; the create() is the authorization only where the response carries an attestation statement that actually signs over `clientDataHash` — `packed` self-attestation, or a platform format doing the same. Under `none`, which is what a platform authenticator returns by default, `attStmt` is empty and *nothing in the response is signed*, so resting a spend on `clientDataJSON` alone would be authorizing against an unsigned string the client supplied. The sidecar therefore requests attestation, verifies the signature, and where it comes back `none` falls back to an immediate `get()` over the same challenge: two prompts, named and counted, rather than a binding that is asserted and not proven. The Transcript records which ceremony carried it (`ceremony = enrollment` vs `assertion`, distinct from the Authority kind itself) so "one prompt" is an assertion the golden replays can check rather than a hope. Sign-count tracked, warns on reset instead of hard-locking. Virtual tap in demo runs identical checks with test keys.
- Buyer Agent: two admission routes, one authority. An allowlisted agent uses Merchant-issued OAuth client-credentials; a stranger self-registers by publishing an Agent Profile at a well-known URL (name, contact, ES256 JWKS) and signing every request against it, and is issued a short token on the spot (ADR-0012). Standards rather than invention: request signatures are RFC 9421 HTTP Message Signatures covering method, target, `Content-Digest` (RFC 9530), `created` and `expires`, with a nonce and a short acceptance window so a captured request cannot be replayed; `agent_id` for a stranger is the RFC 7638 JWK thumbprint. Fetching an attacker-chosen profile URL is an SSRF sink, so the fetcher is hardened by rule: HTTPS only, public IP ranges only (loopback, RFC 1918, link-local and 169.254.169.254 refused), resolve-then-pin against DNS rebinding, no cross-host redirects, hard size and timeout caps, and its own rate limit — a registration attempt is a stranger making the sidecar issue an outbound request. One exception exists and it is deliberately narrow: a dev-mode named-host allowlist (`OPENSTORE_DEV_PROFILE_HOSTS`, host[:port] entries, never a CIDR and never a blanket "allow private") lets the compose demo admit its own chat at `buyer-chat:3001`, because the alternative is a self-registration story nobody can run. It is loud and it is fenced: the cloud metadata address stays refused unconditionally even in dev, resolve-then-pin and the redirect, size and timeout caps still apply, every fetch that used the exception logs a warning and is flagged in health output and the `/agentic` banner, and the sidecar refuses to boot when the allowlist is non-empty alongside live provider keys — the same refusal that already guards live keys in demo mode. A bypass that is silent, broad, or bootable in production is how bypasses reach production. Both get the same four scopes (`search / build-basket / start-checkout / confirm`), pinned ES256, short expiry. No tier unlocks money: `confirm` without a fresh accepted Authority is rejected by the Gate regardless of admission route. Reputation buys throughput only — self-registered agents sit in the low rate-limit tier, allowlisted agents in the high one, blocklisted profiles are refused registration. `agent_id` is the profile key thumbprint for strangers, the client ID for allowlisted agents; both revocable, both on the order. Name badge only — never buys.
- Permissions: `Always allow` covers reads/drafts only; any cart-with-spend, checkout, or place-order needs a fresh Allow-once modal plus a fresh tap (ADR-0008). Gate requires both identities valid; either revokes independently.
- Tap delivery: agent shows `Tap to approve ₹X` → same-domain `/agentic/approve?t=<one-time-token, 5-min, single-use>` → optional `Have a code?` field for `private` Discount Codes, which re-calls `quote`, re-renders the new total, and rebinds before anything is signed (the code never transits the agent, ADR-0015) → the Authority over the *post-application* total: a UPI intent or collect the Consumer approves in their own PSP app (`upi-pin`, the default) or a biometric over the post-application `cart_hash` (`passkey`) — the page is the display of record either way, and any change after render invalidates the token → auto-return via a resume URL carrying an unguessable, single-use, session-bound resume token that resolves to `order_id` + `chat_thread_id` server-side — never those two as bare URL parameters, which would let anyone holding the link resume someone else's checkout, with cart + chat state intact. Sidecar restores the cart snapshot; the chat restores its thread.

## 8. Who signs what

Merchant (via sidecar-held keys): per-item attestation, policy, the Quote (breakdown + total + ETA) + `cart_hash`, evidence root — auto-stamped, no human per sale. Keys are generated in the sidecar, never leave it, rotate additively by `kid`, and are recoverable only from the Merchant's own encrypted enrollment export (ADR-0014). Consumer: one fresh Authority per bag, of a kind the Merchant enabled, carrying its Binding (ADR-0017) — a UPI PIN entered in the payer's own app by default, a per-domain passkey enrollment plus tap where the stronger cart binding is wanted. Agent: nothing authoritative. Provider: HMAC webhooks + dashboard truth for the money-moved fact only (verified, deduped, reconciled); amount/currency mismatch vs order → `failed` + `RELEASE` + `amount-mismatch`, never auto-capture. V1 ships fake + Razorpay behind `make-link / check-status / cancel / refund`; Justpay/Airpay later in the same shape. Each adapter declares its supported method set at boot and the Merchant enables a subset in `/agentic`; anything outside the enabled set refuses with `method-not-supported` naming what *is* enabled (ADR-0013). Razorpay ships declaring UPI, with cards and netbanking enableable without touching the money core. Agent-held delegated payment credentials are refused by design (ADR-0008). Refund and shop-reject are both clicked in demo-admin and both call the sidecar over HMAC rather than writing the Merchant row directly — the sidecar holds the `RESERVE`, so a local write would strand a Ledger hold, and routing through it serialises shop-reject against a Consumer tap and the expiry sweep on one `set-status` key. Refund dialog: reason + amount + per-line restock-to-sale; the admin server calls sidecar refund over HMAC with an amount and per-line restock flags, and sidecar runs Ledger `REFUND` + provider `refund` + conditional `restock` + `set-status`. A Provider-reported dispute or reversal posts a `REVERSAL` Ledger entry and a Timeline event — money moved by someone else is still money moved, and the escrow-zero invariant must close over it. List publisher: list file only. Demo keys are test-marked; sidecar refuses live keys at boot.

## 9. Protocols + proof (Q47/Q52)

MCP live in demo chat. UCP/ACP/AP2 are thin translators over the same core with one golden end-to-end replay each (search → allow → cart → tap → fake-UPI → receipt → verify), core Transcript pinned byte-identical in CI (envelopes differ, core decision bytes don't). Completion posture (deliberate, and in-spec rather than deviant): foreign direct-checkout-inside-AI (UCP-native / ACP-instant) maps to our same-domain approve handoff, which is what UCP's own buyer escalation and its embedded binding already describe — every spend still terminates in a fresh Authority at `/agentic/approve`. What we decline is the trust tier that completes without the buyer, not conformance. Completion without any handoff is phase-two via mandates (ADR-0017). Payment deviation: translators map any instrument outside the Merchant's enabled method set to `method-not-supported` naming what is enabled (ADR-0013) — so a card attempt refuses on a UPI-only store and succeeds on one that enabled cards — and an agent-held delegated payment credential is refused on purpose rather than unimplemented. Demo header toggle `[MCP|UCP|ACP|AP2]` replays the same flow through the selected envelope plus conformance badge. The badge names its deviations inline — capability supported / redirect-only completion, and which payment instruments this Merchant has enabled — so it never reads as unqualified conformance; S6 golden replays assert the deviation text is present. UCP's totals breakdown (`subtotal / items_discount / fulfillment / tax / total`) maps 1:1 onto the Quote, so translators carry no pricing special cases. No second money path anywhere.

## 10. Mounting + consoles (Q43/Q46/Q51)

Compose: `store:3000` + `sidecar:8000` + reverse proxy (proxy owns TLS; dev ships a reference `Caddyfile`). `/ → store`, `/.well-known/agent-commerce.json + /.well-known/ucp.json + /.well-known/jwks.json + /agent + /agentic → sidecar`. One origin (passkeys happy), separate containers/deploys. Demo-admin = shop ops only (catalog/stock/orders). Sidecar console at `/agentic` = keys (enroll/rotate/revoke/export)/policy CRUD/provider + enabled methods/exposure/receipts/agent allowlist + blocklist/attribution; Exposure = which policies are public to agents. Attribution = agent-sourced orders and revenue split by `agent_id` over a date range, read from orders the sidecar already tags — the number that tells a Merchant whether any of this is working. `order.changed` is consumed by the sidecar only; the chat never gets pushed PII — it polls order status through its closed `order status` action. Consumer- and Merchant-facing notifications (order confirmation with receipt link, refund notice, new-order alert) are sent by the Merchant system off its own status changes, because it already owns customer comms and the Contact Point plaintext; the sidecar sends no email or SMS.

## 11. Smoothness law (Q53)

Convenience is sidecar-engineered, never agent-hoped: verbatim signed totals, ordered results, one `approve_url` (5-min single-use), expiry countdown, resume links (an unguessable, single-use, session-bound token resolving server-side; exact state restored on both sides), errors naming the exact fix. Golden replays assert one Allow-once + one tap per spend with zero re-search/re-login and exact resumed state. Inconvenient = red build.

## 12. Guardrails (law from commit one)

Closed sets enforced both directions by build break, with one authoritative registry — `docs/CODES.md`, generated from the code enum and diffed in CI — covering reason codes, scopes, statuses, routes, tool names, and Ledger entry kinds. A code named in prose but missing from the registry is a red build; prose is where closed sets quietly stop being closed. Fail loud, no coercion/defaults/silent fallbacks; every denial carries a closed reason code into signed evidence. No LLM output in the money path; agents are proposal-only and hold no spending authority — a self-registered agent signs its own requests (ADR-0012) but carries no payment credential, and `confirm` without a fresh accepted Authority is refused regardless (ADR-0017). Money integers (paise), time UTC. Server recomputes totals, hashes, discounts, and caps — never trusts agent supply. Agent-facing order reads and refund requests resolve only against orders carrying that `agent_id`, and an order belonging to someone else is indistinguishable from one that does not exist — admission is open by design (ADR-0012), so every stranger holds these scopes and an unscoped order read is an enumeration oracle over other Consumers' order state. Least-privilege per tool/scope, signed + pinned tool/manifest definitions, idempotency (`order_id:attempt`) on every mutation, atomic compare-and-set on stock (never negative), replay-safe webhooks + events (`event_id` dedupe, out-of-order safe, reconciler poll), single-Merchant scoping on every query. Rate limits are per agent tier and per IP on every `/agent/*` route with a separate throttle on tap-token issuance, approve attempts, and Discount Code attempts — every wrong code refuses as the same `code-invalid` with no message or timing tell, since door 9 otherwise answers "is this a code?" all day; policy caps bound an order, limits bound an attacker. PII is committed to by hash in evidence and stored in plaintext only in the Merchant row (ADR-0011). Every money-path decision emits a structured log line (order, agent, consumer, reason code, duration) alongside the Transcript — Transcripts are forensic, logs are operational.

## 13. Explicitly out (phase two, same core untouched)

Campaigns/rules/bots/stalled-loops, hosted mall/search/ranking, multi-Merchant tenancy, multi-location, cancellation and restocking fees, store credit, bulk cancel, auto-fulfilment, shipping labels, subscriptions, multi-currency and international duties, Consumer accounts and a self-serve returns portal, fraud scoring, abandoned-cart recovery, Merkle batch-anchoring, OMS write paths beyond the 9-door trait. Manual prices and a hand-entered code table only — no rule engine anywhere. Preorder/backorder is not a cut so much as a consequence already made elsewhere: stock is `int ≥ 0` with `CHECK (available >= 0)` and v1 never oversells (§5), so selling against future stock would need a second inventory model, not a flag — same reasoning as auto-fulfilment. Staff roles inside one Merchant's own admin (shop-ops-only vs. refund/pricing-capable) are a real, common ask past solo-founder scale and are explicitly deferred rather than assumed away — `demo-admin` stays single-operator (PLAN-merchant-site.md M2) until that gets its own grill.

One exclusion has a reason worth writing down, because it looks like an omission and is not (COD was the other, and is now **in** via ADR-0018: the Ledger holds stock and money separately, so an order holding no money is expressible and the cash path writes a `CAPTURE` with no preceding `RESERVE`. No money is held across a COD delivery — UPI has no primitive for it, Reserve Pay is a prepaid reserve rather than the authorize-then-capture it was taken for, and OpenStore holding the funds itself is refused by ADR-0021 and ADR-0023. ADR-0018 keeps that gap open with named candidates rather than closing it):
- **Delegated agent-held payment credentials** (Shop Pay tokens, ACP shared payment tokens) are refused on purpose: they are exactly the authority we removed from agents (ADR-0008, ADR-0013). Their absence is the product, not a gap. It costs us native in-agent completion on the gated surfaces and we pay it (ADR-0016); the Indian regime favours the tap above the small-value delegation band (additional-factor requirements on card-not-present spend, tokenisation rules for stored credentials, a UPI PIN entered by the payer in the payer's own app) while NPCI's unlaunched Unified Agent Protocol is building sanctioned delegation below it — a bank-side, capped, revocable delegation that would reach us as a Provider method (ADR-0013, ADR-0016) rather than as an agent-held credential. Where the line falls is a matter for a legal opinion, not an argument this document gets to win.

Reach is deliberately absent from v1 and is not the same kind of exclusion. A conformant sidecar no agent can see is not a product, so the permissionless doors — an ingestible Product Feed and JSON-LD, an MCP endpoint any client can add, a second trait implementation distributed where merchants already install things — are specified as a phase-two track in [`PLAN-distribution.md`](./PLAN-distribution.md), gated on install (`PLAN.md` step 7) and changing nothing in the money core.

## 14. Operations (what a Merchant lives with after install)

- **Install**: one command. `openstore up <domain>` writes the compose file, the reference `Caddyfile`, and a fresh `.env` from the template, generates the signing key, prints the encrypted key export to save, and prints the `/agentic` first-run URL. A Merchant who can point DNS can run it; anything needing hand-edited YAML before first boot is a bug in this gate.
- **Keys**: enroll / rotate / revoke / export in `/agentic`, per ADR-0014. Rotation is additive and never re-signs history; revocation invalidates the future, not the past. First-run refuses to continue until the export is acknowledged as saved.
- **Health + observability**: `/healthz` + `/readyz` on every service; structured money-path logs (order, agent, consumer, reason code, duration, never secrets or PII plaintext); counters for gate refusals by reason code, authority outcomes by kind, provider webhook lag, reconciler drift, and holds still open past their deadline — the sidecar owns both expiry clocks and the Merchant may not self-expire (§5), so a wedged or stopped sidecar holds stock indefinitely and the only defence is that somebody can see it: `/agentic` lists overdue holds and the documented release path goes through the sidecar, never a Merchant-side write. A Merchant at 2am needs *what is failing and why*, which the Transcript alone does not answer.
- **Abuse**: per-tier and per-IP rate limits on `/agent/*`, a separate throttle on tap-token issuance and approve attempts, and Merchant allowlist/blocklist over Agent Profiles (ADR-0012).
- **Disputes**: Provider-reported disputes and reversals post a `REVERSAL` Ledger entry plus a Timeline event and never silently vanish, whatever the rail produced them — on UPI an adjudicated complaint, a post-settlement adjustment or a bank correction rather than an issuer chargeback, and the adapter carries the dispute's turnaround deadline because a Merchant needs to know when evidence is owed; the sealed bundle is the Merchant's evidence pack and can be handed over without PII (ADR-0011).
- **Data retention**: Destination and Contact Point plaintext live only in the Merchant order row with a Merchant-set retention window and an erasure action; evidence commits by hash, so erasure never breaks verification (ADR-0011). DPDP obligations sit with the Merchant, who is the data fiduciary; the sidecar's job is to not make compliance impossible.
