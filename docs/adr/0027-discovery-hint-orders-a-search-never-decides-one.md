# The card's discovery hint orders a search and never decides one

A buyer agent holding ten shops had one way to answer "which of these sells mugs": ask all ten. The tool loop fans an unaddressed read out to every known shop and the model learned a shop existed only by seeing its domain come back in a result, so every question started with ten round trips and the shopper watched eight `not-found` cards go past for a question nobody had. The obvious fix — let a Merchant say what it sells, and let the agent narrow to the shops that match — is also the obvious way to build something worse: a self-declared field used as a filter answers "they do not stock that" about stock that is there, and a wrong answer given quickly is worse than a right answer given slowly. This ADR adds the field and fixes what it may be used for.

`agent-commerce.json` gains `merchant.description` and `merchant.categories`, declared by the Merchant through `OPENSTORE_MERCHANT_DESCRIPTION` and `OPENSTORE_MERCHANT_CATEGORIES`, and a `merchant.note` that states in the document itself that the two are unverified and are not grounds for concluding absence. The note is part of the contract rather than a line in prose somewhere: this card is what a stranger integrates against without reading the repository, and a hint whose limits are documented only where integrators will not look is a hint that will be used as a filter. They are **declared, never derived**. A summary computed from the catalogue would be truthful in a way a declaration cannot be, and it would couple the one document discovery cannot do without to the trait service — a shop whose catalogue was briefly down would become a shop no agent could find, trading a cheap wrong-order problem for an expensive cannot-be-reached one. Both keys are absent, not blank, when a Merchant declares nothing: an empty string is a claim that the shop said something.

What this rules in: an agent ordering which shops to ask, and asking one or two first. What it rules out, on the consuming side as much as the publishing side: treating a non-match as an answer, reporting "not stocked" without a search that said so, and narrowing without widening when the narrow pass comes back empty. The demo makes the failure mode concrete rather than hypothetical — four SKUs deliberately sit in two shops at once, and PantryLine stocks filter coffee while calling itself a grocery and Kettle & Grain stocks it while calling itself kitchenware. A filter built on either description gets one of them wrong. The buyer chat therefore hands the model a roster of shops with their declared lines and the same warning the card carries, and leaves the narrowing to a judgment the model can make and revise, rather than to a matcher in the loop that would silently drop shops with no way for anybody to see it happen.

Consequences: a Merchant can make itself easier to find and cannot make itself authoritative, because nothing downstream is permitted to treat the claim as a fact. The cost is that an agent following this correctly sometimes pays for two passes instead of one — the likely shops, then the rest — and that cost is the point: it is what keeps a wrong hint expensive in round trips rather than expensive in wrong answers. A future Listing Index (ADR-0022) filters on category and region as the Listing format carries them; this field is the same claim published on the Merchant's own card rather than to an Index, and it inherits the same rule — filtering a lookup is the Index's job, deciding a shop has nothing is still only a search's.
