# A Registrar can enrol on rails a self-hosted merchant cannot, and it earns that role by holding nothing

ADR-0024 ends on a structural limit rather than a technical one: the agentic payment rails that exist admit *enrolled participants*. Visa Intelligent Commerce admits agent platforms; NPCI's UAP will verify and authorise agents inside the UPI ecosystem, and the RBI is holding it precisely over who is accountable when an agent goes wrong. A sidecar that any merchant installs on their own domain, answering to nobody by design (ADR-0007, ADR-0012), cannot enrol in either, and no amount of better code changes that. Autonomy arrives through a door that requires a name on the paperwork.

`docs/REVIEW-findings.md` K1 framed the strategic question as two options — become a hosted multi-tenant platform, or accept ADR-0016's reach ceiling permanently — and concluded the two are different companies. The rail-enrolment finding opens a third that K1 did not consider, because it assumed centralisation means *operating merchants' deployments*. It does not have to. An entity can be the accountable, enrolled participant on a rail while operating no merchant's store, holding no merchant's keys, and touching no consumer's money.

Decision: that entity is a **Registrar**, and it is defined by four walls it may never cross. Each wall is load-bearing for a decision already taken, and crossing any one of them converts the Registrar into a different regulated thing:

- **No funds.** Never receives, pools, or routes payment. Pooling is the single fact that turns a payment gateway into an RBI-authorised payment aggregator (ADR-0023) and is the trigger for GST TCS liability as an Electronic Commerce Operator (ADR-0021). Settlement continues to run Consumer → Merchant's own Provider account, untouched.
- **No keys.** Never holds a merchant signing key it can use. It may hold an encrypted export it cannot decrypt, because that is a filing cabinet rather than custody, and it would close the key-loss gap (MER-1) at no cost to ADR-0014's self-custody premise. The decryption passphrase never leaves the merchant.
- **No consumer PII.** Never receives Destination, Contact Point, payer handle, or per-transaction data. SPEC §14's entire DPDP fiduciary allocation rests on OpenStore receiving nothing from any deployment; a Registrar that sees orders collapses it.
- **No ranking, curation, or placement.** ADR-0022 forecloses this for the Index and the reasoning transfers unchanged: a participant that can promote or suppress is a mall regardless of what it charges.

Inside those walls the Registrar does four things, in order of why it exists. **Rail enrolment**: it is the named, vetted participant on UAP, on a card-network agentic programme, or on whatever succeeds them, so that a mandate-authorised purchase can reach a merchant who never enrolled in anything. **Agent attestation**: it vouches that a given `agent_id` is a known, enrolled agent rather than a self-registered stranger — which the rails will require and ADR-0012 deliberately does not provide. **Directory**: the Reference Index of ADR-0022, unchanged and still non-privileged. **Operational services that hold nothing**: the encrypted-export filing cabinet above, and the alerting channel MER-3 wants, both of which work on opaque blobs and health signals rather than on commerce data.

## The tension, stated rather than finessed

Enrolment means accountability, and accountability means a lever. A rail that admits the Registrar will expect it to be able to *revoke* a misbehaving agent — that is the whole substance of what RBI is currently holding UAP over. A revocable lever is a chokepoint, and ADR-0022 exists to forbid exactly that.

Both cannot be true at once, and the resolution is to stop pretending one thing is being built. **The Index and the Registrar are separate, and only one of them is load-bearing.** The Index stays a phonebook: mirrorable, unranked, no accept/reject step, no OpenStore-only feature, exactly as ADR-0022 specifies. The Registrar is load-bearing by construction, because a rail will not accept an accountable party that cannot act, and saying otherwise would be a lie told to make an architecture diagram look cleaner. They should be separable in operation and, if the liability analysis says so, in legal entity.

What follows is a two-tier outcome, and it should be stated plainly rather than discovered later:

- **The permissionless tier is the baseline and stays forever.** Any merchant self-hosts, any agent self-registers, no one's approval is required, every spend terminates in a human tap. Nothing in this ADR narrows it, and a merchant who never touches a Registrar loses nothing they have today.
- **The registered tier is additive and optional.** A merchant who opts in becomes reachable by mandate-authorised agents on rails that require an enrolled counterparty. The price is a relationship with an entity that can revoke, which is the rails' price and not ours to waive.

A merchant choosing the first tier forgoes autonomous-agent traffic. That is a real cost, honestly the same cost ADR-0016 already accepts, and it is the merchant's to weigh rather than ours to decide by making the choice for them.

## Why this dodges K1's objection, and where it does not

K1's strongest argument against a central play was ADR-0008: per-domain passkey enrolment forfeits the one asset a platform has, a single buyer identity amortised across every merchant. Under a mandate that argument does not bite, because the amortised identity is not ours to build — it already exists in the bank and the rail. The Consumer's relationship is with their own PSP, the mandate lives there with its caps and revocation, and the Registrar never needs an RP ID of its own or a roaming credential. ADR-0008 is untouched, which is the test any proposal in this area has to pass.

Where K1 still bites, and this ADR does not resolve: hosted mode. Operating merchants' sidecars breaks walls two and three simultaneously, changes SPEC §14's fiduciary allocation, and is a different liability class (as `docs/DRAFT-discovery-registry.md` already noted). It is **not** part of this decision, it does not follow from it, and it needs its own ADR and its own legal answer (`docs/COUNSEL-BRIEF.md` Q1(c) already asks the RBI half). A Registrar is not a first step toward hosting and must not be justified as one.

Open questions that go to counsel rather than being settled here, and which should be added to the existing engagement: whether operating a Registrar that attests agents and can revoke them reads as "owns, operates or manages a platform for electronic commerce" under CGST §2(45) — a materially stronger case than the one ADR-0021 answers for the sidecar, and the same question `docs/COUNSEL-BRIEF.md` Q2(c) already raises for the Index, now with a sharper fact pattern; what liability an enrolled Registrar actually carries for the conduct of agents it attested, under each rail's own scheme rules; and whether the encrypted-export filing cabinet creates any custody obligation despite the Registrar being unable to decrypt.

Consequences: no change to v1, which does not have a Registrar and does not need one — the Gate, the closed Authority set, ADR-0012's open admission and ADR-0022's phonebook all stand exactly as written. What this ADR fixes is that the *option* is now bounded before it is wanted, so the first real rail conversation is a decision against stated walls rather than an improvisation under commercial pressure. Two things become explicit obligations on any future Registrar work: the Index must remain independently mirrorable so that a load-bearing Registrar never makes the directory load-bearing with it, and the permissionless tier must keep working end to end with no Registrar present, verified the way any other invariant is. K1 stays open — this narrows it from "hosted platform or nothing" to "hosted platform, Registrar, or nothing," and the hosted question is unchanged by this ADR.
