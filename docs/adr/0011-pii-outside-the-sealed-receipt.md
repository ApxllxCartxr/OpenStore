# Contact and address live outside the sealed receipt

The receipt is immutable by design (ADR-0009) and Indian DPDP erasure rights are not. Rather than choose, the bundle commits to PII instead of carrying it: the `bought` section stores `HMAC-SHA256(order_salt, canonical destination bytes)` and the same over the canonical contact bytes — never the street address, name, email, or phone.

The salt is the whole mechanism, so it never leaves the Merchant order row and is never written into the bundle. A plain digest of this data would be theatre: a phone number is 10^10 candidates and an address is enumerable from a pincode, so a published bundle carrying its own salt would hand back exactly the PII the erasure was meant to remove. With the salt held only in the deletable row, erasure destroys the opening and the commitment becomes permanently unopenable. A keyed MAC rather than `sha256(salt || bytes)` also buys domain separation and no length-extension footgun, for free.

Consequences: the receipt viewer renders destination and contact by re-reading the Merchant row, recomputing with its salt, and comparing to the pinned commitment — `erased` when the row is gone, `tampered` when it disagrees. This binding is therefore an *online* check; offline verification (ADR-0009) still covers the chain, the signatures, and every non-PII fact, and reports the PII sections as `unopened` rather than failing. Evidence bundles can be handed to a Payment Provider or a dispute desk without leaking PII. Retention policy (SPEC §14) is a Merchant setting, not a sidecar default.
