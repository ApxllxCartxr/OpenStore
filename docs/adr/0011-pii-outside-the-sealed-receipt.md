# Contact and address live outside the sealed receipt

The receipt is immutable by design (ADR-0009) and Indian DPDP erasure rights are not. Rather than choose, the bundle commits to PII instead of carrying it: the `bought` section stores `HMAC-SHA256(order_salt, canonical destination bytes)` and the same over the canonical contact bytes — never the street address, name, email, or phone.

The salt is the whole mechanism, so it never leaves the Merchant order row and is never written into the bundle. A plain digest of this data would be theatre: a phone number is 10^10 candidates and an address is enumerable from a pincode, so a published bundle carrying its own salt would hand back exactly the PII the erasure was meant to remove. With the salt held only in the deletable row, erasure destroys the opening and the commitment becomes permanently unopenable. A keyed MAC rather than `sha256(salt || bytes)` also buys domain separation and no length-extension footgun, for free.

## Where the plaintext lives during a checkout

"Never leaves the Merchant order row" is a rule about storage, not about transit: the sidecar necessarily handles a Destination in memory on its way to door 9, and reading it as a prohibition would send an implementer off to invent an encryption scheme for a value that is about to be posted anyway.

Storage is where it binds, and `quote-fresh` is the case that tests it. The Gate re-calls door 9 with identical inputs immediately before `RESERVE` (SPEC §4), and door 9 takes a Destination — so at that moment the sidecar either retained the plaintext across the ceremony or reads it back. It reads it back, through door 7, on every re-quote. Retention would need a stated lifetime and a scrub point and would quietly make the sidecar a second place this data lives, which is the thing this decision exists to prevent; one extra call on a path that already makes several is the cheaper half of that trade. The consequence to expect is that an erased order can no longer be re-quoted, which is correct — an erased order is terminal and has nothing left to price.

## The Payer Handle and `consumer_id`

ADR-0017 makes `upi-pin` the default authority, which introduces a third piece of Consumer PII: the payer handle the Provider reports, a UPI VPA in v1. It is the same class as Destination and Contact Point and gets the same treatment — plaintext only in the Merchant order row, under the same retention window and the same erasure action. The Merchant needs it there regardless, because a refund goes back to source.

What reaches the Transcript, the evidence and the attribution panel is `consumer_id`, and it is a keyed pseudonym rather than the handle: `HMAC(deploy_pseudonym_key, payer_handle)`. The key is deploy-level and deliberately *not* the `order_salt` — a per-order salt would make every order a different Consumer and destroy the one property this identifier exists for. Under `passkey` the pseudonym is already implicit, since a credential is scoped to the RP ID, so this makes the two kinds agree rather than diverge: a repeat buyer links within one Merchant and is unlinkable across Merchants.

The caveat this ADR makes about bare digests applies to the key with equal force. A VPA is `phone@psp` and therefore enumerable, so a leaked `deploy_pseudonym_key` hands back the handles it was protecting. It is a signing-grade secret, lives with the signing key and its custody rules (ADR-0014), and rotating it breaks the link between old and new orders — the correct trade, and a stated consequence rather than a surprise the first time someone rotates it.

Consequences: the receipt viewer renders destination and contact by re-reading the Merchant row, recomputing with its salt, and comparing to the pinned commitment — `erased` when the row is gone, `tampered` when it disagrees. This binding is therefore an *online* check; offline verification (ADR-0009) still covers the chain, the signatures, and every non-PII fact, and reports the PII sections as `unopened` rather than failing. Evidence bundles can be handed to a Payment Provider or a dispute desk without leaking PII. Retention policy (SPEC §14) is a Merchant setting, not a sidecar default.
